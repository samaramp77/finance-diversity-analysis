The short version

Compare the total, then check the typical program.

The SEC leads the four Power conferences by total modeled market value. The Big Ten’s larger membership helps explain why its total is close behind. A median program estimate gives a different view of conference depth than a single league-wide total.

04 / SPORTS

Football drives every Power 4 market—but not equally.

Football makes up the largest share in each Power 4 conference. The Big 12 has the lowest football share of the four, while men’s basketball takes its largest slice. Women’s basketball is a smaller modeled category in every conference, with the SEC showing the highest share among the four.

“Everything else” is the source’s grouped category; it should not be read as zero activity in individual sports.

05 / LEADERS

The top program is not always in the top conference.

Texas is the SEC leader at $76.9M, while Ohio State leads the Big Ten at $67.2M. Miami leads the ACC at $72.9M, and Texas Tech leads the Big 12 at $58.1M. Conference totals answer one question; individual program concentration answers another.

Leader values are modeled estimates for the highest-valued program listed within each Power 4 conference.

06 / POSITION GROUPS

Football’s money is concentrated in a few position groups.

In a separate FBS-football model, quarterbacks are fewer than 5% of tracked players but account for about 20% of modeled market value. Wide receivers account for about 19%, while edge rushers and cornerbacks form the next tier. The median quarterback is modeled at $585K, compared with $57K for a wide receiver and $36K for a long snapper.

This is a position-value lens for 138 FBS football programs—not a claim about actual pay and not a complete Division I sport-by-sport position census.

The important limit

This is a market model, not a payroll.

The public NCAA NIL Assist dashboard reports de-identified disclosure aggregates, but it does not currently expose a consistent public SEC-versus-Big-Ten table. This project uses The Sideline’s D-I roster-market estimates for the named-conference comparison and keeps that distinction visible.

Data notes

What this project counts—and what it does not.

This project uses The Sideline’s NIL by Conference directory, captured September 30, 2026. The 32 conference rows used here represent 354 NCAA Division I programs and 49,842 athletes. It describes the figures as estimates and says they combine public valuation benchmarks with model estimates.

“Combined market” is the sum of program-level roster estimates for the selected conference. “Median program” is the middle program estimate within that conference. The sport breakdown is available for the Power 4 and groups smaller categories under “Everything else.” The position-group section uses termiNIL’s FBS football position analysis as a separate, narrower model.

  • D-I only: no Division II or Division III conferences are included in the conference ranking.
  • Named conferences: SEC, Big Ten, ACC, Big 12, and all other conferences listed by the source are directly comparable within the same model.
  • Position scope: position groups cover FBS football only; they do not represent every D-I sport or every D-I conference.
  • Not contracts: values are not verified salaries, reported deals, conference distributions, or guaranteed athlete pay.
  • Not NCAA data: the NCAA dashboard is linked for context, but its reported disclosures are not combined with these modeled estimates.
  • Snapshot, not trend: the sources can revise estimates as rosters and valuation assumptions change.
