Where does the money go?

This report compares modeled 2026 roster-market estimates across all 32 Division I conferences, then places that market beside public NCAA NIL disclosure snapshots from 2024 and 2025. The central finding is that the Power 4 holds most of the modeled market, but a total can hide how much the typical program or athlete differs.

The short version

Compare the total, then check the typical program.

The SEC leads the four Power conferences by total modeled market value. The Big Ten’s larger membership helps explain why its total is close behind. A median program estimate gives a different view of conference depth than a single league-wide total, while the public disclosure snapshots show that average deal values can move differently from medians.

06 / POSITION GROUPS

Football’s money is concentrated in a few position groups.

In a separate FBS-football model, quarterbacks are fewer than 5% of tracked players but account for about 20% of modeled market value. Wide receivers account for about 19%, while edge rushers and cornerbacks form the next tier. The median quarterback is modeled at $585K, compared with $57K for a wide receiver and $36K for a long snapper.

This is a position-value lens for 138 FBS football programs—not a claim about actual pay and not a complete Division I sport-by-sport position census.

The important limit

This is a market model, not a payroll.

The public NCAA NIL Assist dashboard reports de-identified disclosure aggregates, while The Sideline’s conference directory supplies modeled roster-market estimates. They answer different questions and are shown as separate layers instead of being combined into one fake salary total.

Data notes

What this project counts—and what it does not.

The primary modeled dataset comes from The Sideline’s NIL by Conference directory, captured September 30, 2026. One row represents one of the 32 Division I conferences and includes its rank, group/tier, program count, median program estimate, combined market estimate, market share, and largest listed program. The rows represent 354 programs. No conference rows were dropped after validation.

The historical layer comes from the NCAA NIL Assist public dashboard. Its 2024 and 2025 rows are de-identified disclosure snapshots, not the same thing as the modeled 2026 conference market. The position-group section uses termiNIL’s FBS football position analysis as a separate, narrower model.

  • Market total: sum of the program-level roster-market estimates across all conference rows.
  • Market share: a conference’s market estimate divided by the all-D-I market total, multiplied by 100.
  • Power 4 share: the four Power 4 conference estimates summed, divided by the all-D-I total, multiplied by 100.
  • SEC edge: SEC market divided by Big Ten market, minus 1, multiplied by 100.
  • Sport mix: each modeled sport estimate divided by that conference’s modeled total, multiplied by 100.
  • Position share: each position group’s modeled market divided by the FBS position-model total, multiplied by 100.
  • Historical changes: 2025 minus 2024, divided by 2024, multiplied by 100. The date ranges are shown because they are not identical full-year periods.
  • Player builder: conference average equals market divided by programs; the displayed lift is the square root of conference average divided by all-D-I average, multiplied by the sport/position baseline.
  • Scope: values are not verified contracts, guaranteed pay, salaries, conference distributions, or collective budgets. The NCAA dashboard’s public disclosure categories and the modeled conference estimates remain separate.
