Race, gender, and the climb

Who gets to move up?

A decade of public workforce data reveals a finance industry where women are a majority overall—but representation changes as the job ladder gets taller. This project looks closely at Black women’s place in that climb, state by state and role by role.

The short version

The ladder is not one story.

Representation at the bottom and middle of an industry can look very different from representation at the top. These data do not follow individual women through a career, and they do not measure pay. They do let us ask a clear question: who is present in which job categories, in which places, and how does that mix change over time?

01 / THE BIG PICTURE

Women are the majority overall, but leadership is nearly even.

In 2023, women made up 59.12% of the non-suppressed finance and insurance workforce counts. In the combined senior and mid-level officials/managers categories, women made up 49.56%. The difference is not a claim about a single company; it is a national aggregate across the tidy public-use file.

02 / BLACK WOMEN

Black women’s presence is cut roughly in half at the leadership rung.

Black women represented 9.38% of the non-suppressed finance workforce counts in 2023, but 4.81% of the non-suppressed senior and mid-level leadership counts. That gap is the central finding of this project: a workforce can be diverse in total while the top of the ladder remains less representative.

The EEOC file reports aggregate counts with disclosure protection. These are representation measures, not individual-level career outcomes.

03 / JOB CATEGORIES

The pipeline changes shape at every level.

Women represented 77.43% of administrative-support workers, 51.54% of professionals, and 34.53% of senior officials and managers. Looking only at “women in finance” hides these distinct job-category patterns.

04 / TIME

Leadership representation moved within a narrow band across the decade.

Black women’s share of non-suppressed senior and mid-level leadership counts was 4.70% in 2014 and 4.81% in 2023, with a low of 4.32% in 2021 and a high of 5.32% in 2015. Their overall workforce share stayed close to 10%. The jump in the total workforce series after 2021 is visible and should be treated as a data-coverage or reporting change, not automatically as a sudden hiring wave.

05 / REGIONS

The South has the highest Black-women representation in this file.

In 2023, Black women represented 14.07% of the South’s finance workforce, compared with 3.41% in the West. Regional context matters because finance employment is connected to population, industry mix, and where large employers are located.

06 / THE TOP OF THE LADDER

White employees account for 70.59% of leadership in 2023.

White men represented 36.56% and White women 34.03% of the non-suppressed leadership aggregate. Black women represented 4.81%. This is a composition story: the categories show who occupies leadership, not why the pattern exists.

07 / PLACE

State context can change the picture completely.

Black women’s share ranges from 22.82% in Mississippi to 0.00% in Wyoming in the 2023 state-level file. These extremes should be read alongside the number of establishments and the size of the finance workforce; small-state percentages can be volatile.

08 / SCOPE

A decade gives us a pattern, not a perfect before-and-after.

The file contains 71,400 tidy rows representing 51 states, 10 job categories, 7 race/ethnicity categories, 2 sex categories, and 10 years. The 2022–2023 totals are higher than prior years, so the most defensible comparisons are within a year, within a job category, and alongside the documented coverage change.

Data notes

What this project counts—and what it does not.

This project uses the official EEOC EEO-1 Public Use Files for 2014–2023. The EEO-1 collection covers private employers with 100 or more employees and certain federal contractors. The public files are aggregate and confidentiality-protected.

One published row is one state × year × job category × race/ethnicity × sex aggregate for NAICS sector 52, Finance and Insurance. I keep the 51 state-level finance-sector records for each year, reshape the demographic/job columns into tidy rows, and drop all other geography levels, industries, and aggregate race/sex rows. No individual employee records appear in the website.

  • Annual, job, and dashboard shares use the non-suppressed employee_count values in the tidy file as numerator and denominator.
  • State and region Black-women shares use the Black-women count over the source row’s one unique finance_total all-workforce denominator; this limits suppression-driven state distortions.
  • Leadership = Senior officials and managers + Mid-level officials and managers.
  • Leadership composition = each race/sex leadership count ÷ all non-suppressed leadership counts × 100.
  • Blank/suppressed source cells remain blank and are excluded from tidy totals.
